Nine desktop characters · Windows · MIT

The BusyBusiness Bits

They live in little framed windows on your desktop, they speak in garbled synthesised nonsense, and they will audit your disk, catch the traceback you just copied, and file what landed in Downloads.

They are not nine chatbots.
Each one owns exactly one verb and the tools that verb implies. The Reaper can delete things; he cannot touch your calendar. The Secretary owns the calendar; she cannot delete anything.

The one rule

One Bit, one verb.

Boss measureCoder buildCourier moveInvestigator digReaper cutSecretary scheduleWizard orchestrateGhost rememberLibrarian file

If two of them could plausibly own a job, the job is split wrong. That constraint is the whole design — it's what makes it obvious who to ask, and it's why nine characters isn't nine times the noise.

It runs on the same split underneath. A tool belongs to exactly one character, and asking the wrong one gets you told:

“that isn't your job — delete_paths belongs to The Reaper.”

The roster · 51 tools

Nine of them. No overlap.

The Boss

measure

Wants the number before the opinion. Reads your spreadsheets, tracks what moved, and holds the approval gate every other Bit passes through.

analyse_table, metrics_pulse, track_metric, pending_approvals, approve, refuse

The Coder

build

Three monitors and a cold drink. Catches the traceback the moment you copy it, and wants the actual error, the actual file, the actual version.

read_clipboard, git_status, check_python, repo_health, run_shell ⚿, write_file ⚿

The Courier

move

Never sits down. Sorts what lands in Downloads by rule, carries files between your machines, and drafts the follow-up you keep not sending.

triage_downloads, draft_email, file_by_rule ⚿, deliver_file ⚿, open_path ⚿

The Investigator

dig

Trench coat, dramatic pause. Reads the web page, extracts the PDF, hunts the log, and builds the dossier before you take the meeting.

fetch_url, read_pdf, search_logs, dossier

The Reaper

cut

Calm, slow, and utterly without sentiment about sunk cost. Finds what's dead on your disk and asks what happens if you simply do not.

disk_audit, find_duplicates, startup_items, stale_apps, list_processes, kill_process ⚿, delete_paths ⚿

The Secretary

schedule

Holds the schedule, the notes and the institutional memory. Turns what was just said into owners and dates, then reminds you what you agreed.

tasks_list, task_add, task_done, morning_brief, end_of_day

The Wizard

orchestrate

Lives in the console and says “Okay… BAM!”. Fires whole routines on one word, switches your desktop between profiles, and summons whoever actually knows.

list_routines, run_routine, system_state, bit_status, install_package ⚿

The Ghost

remember

What's left of everything quietly dropped. Brings up one abandoned thing at an inconvenient moment, and never the same one twice.

stale_scan, open_loops, verdict, mark_surfaced

The Librarian

file

Knows where it was written down and is quietly appalled that you don't. Answers with a path, and tracks which version supersedes which.

index_build, index_search, versions_of, naming_check, file_document ⚿

Permissions

The Boss holds the gate.

He holds the licence in the fiction, so he holds the permission gate in the architecture. The gated tiers do not run when called — they queue an approval that has to be cleared first. That is what makes it safe to leave nine characters running unattended all day.

Read

Free rein. Looks at anything, changes nothing. Investigator, Ghost, Librarian, Boss.

Write

Creates and edits inside its own scope. Reversible by definition. Secretary, Librarian, Coder.

Execute

Shell, installs, system settings. Shows the command before it runs. Coder, Wizard.

Send

Anything leaving the machine. Always previewed. Courier.

Destroy

Proposes only — and deletions move to a dated graveyard rather than vanishing. Reaper, Librarian, Courier.

Coder → run_shell("npm test")
QUEUED FOR APPROVAL as A1. Say it needs the Boss's nod before you can do it. Nothing destructive, outbound or executable happens on its own. Ever.

Ambient

They speak first.

Seven watchers, each owned by one character. The hard part isn't noticing things — it's staying quiet, because anyone who comments on everything gets muted within a day.

clipboard
Coder. You copy a traceback anywhere on the machine and he offers the fix. Ordinary copies are ignored.
downloads
Courier. Something lands, he works out where it belongs.
brief
Secretary. The shape of the day, once — and only if something is actually due.
due
Secretary. A task falls due, or quietly goes past it.
approvals
Boss. Someone's waiting on his nod.
repo
Coder. The build breaks, or work sits uncommitted for days.
haunt
Ghost. One abandoned thing, occasionally — never the same one twice.
the rules
Only summoned Bits speak. Nothing fires twice, even across a restart. One voice at a time.

Routines

The chains are the point.

The value isn't nine assistants, it's what happens when they hand off. The Wizard fires these on one word.

morning
Secretary the shape of the day → Boss the numbers that moved → Ghost one thing you dropped
triage
Courier sorts what landed → Librarian files and indexes it → Reaper flags what shouldn't have been kept
pre-meeting
Secretary agenda and last decisions → Investigator the dossier → Librarian the relevant documents
end of day
Secretary what you promised → Coder what's uncommitted → Ghost what slipped
reckoning
Reaper the cut list → Ghost the open loops → Boss approves or refuses → Secretary schedules the survivors
ship it
Coder builds and commits → Librarian files the artefacts → Courier delivers → Secretary logs it done

What you get

The whole crew, free.

  • Nine characters, each in its own borderless always-on-top window
  • 51 tools across the roster, tiered and gated
  • Seven ambient watchers that know when to stay quiet
  • Per-character voices synthesised from scratch — no audio library
  • Hand-drawn pixel sprites, discovered off disk by filename
  • Any character can be swapped onto your own n8n workflow
